TOPICLENS - PROJECT BRIEF (for design)
=======================================
Last updated: 30 Oct 2026
Copy deck (editable, section by section): https://claude.ai/artifact/MckSDdQBWKuY6x2DLRDu4H


0. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
--------------------
Recommend the most relevant news articles to each user, based on:
  - their reading and clicking history (opening a story counts as a click),
  - their preferred news topics / sections (chosen at sign-up, editable),
  - their interaction with previously recommended articles (stories passed
    over above a click are pushed down: "skip-above" implicit feedback),
  - their explicit feedback (More / Less like this, Not interested, topic
    sliders and mutes).
The recommendation itself comes first; explanations support it (collapsed
"Why am I seeing this?" panel, one-line reason per card).


1. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
-------------------
TopicLens is an explainable news recommender: a trained machine-learning model
picks news stories for you, and every pick shows exactly why it was chosen,
so you can push back and reshape your feed.

Tagline options:
  - A news feed that shows its working.
  - Recommendations you can argue with.
  - Every pick comes with its reasons.


2. WHAT IT IS
-------------
- A university research project (not a commercial news app).
- Built on the paper: Liu, Greene, Li, Jiang & Dong (2024),
  "Topic-Centric Explanations for News Recommendation", ACM Transactions on
  Recommender Systems 3(2). https://doi.org/10.1145/3680295
- Trained and tested on MIND, the Microsoft News Dataset: real, anonymised
  logs of what people were shown on Microsoft News and what they clicked.
- Runs in the browser. No server; your profile stays on your device.


3. THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
------------------------
News apps decide what you read, but never say why. Their models are black
boxes, which makes them hard to trust and impossible to correct.
TopicLens shows the reasons behind every recommendation and lets you change
them (more / less like this, topic sliders, mutes), with a log of what changed.


4. WHO IT'S FOR
---------------
- Curious readers tired of black-box feeds: see the reasons, steer the feed.
- Students learning recommender systems: a working end-to-end example (data,
  features, models, metrics).
- Researchers in explainable AI: explanations that are exact score breakdowns.
- Reviewers / examiners: every number is labelled as measured, from the
  paper, or not yet evaluated.


5. HOW IT WORKS (user-facing, 5 steps)
--------------------------------------
1. Tell us what you read - pick a few sections, or start as a demo reader.
2. Topics are learned from the text - the system finds themes in thousands
   of real headlines on its own.
3. A trained model predicts your clicks - learned from ~157,000 real visits
   to Microsoft News.
4. Every pick explains itself - "Why am I seeing this?" splits the score into
   its parts, shows the past reads behind it and the words that matched.
5. You push back, it adapts - More / Less like this, sliders and mutes change
   the feed at once; every change to your top 10 is logged.

7. THE MODELS
-------------
All trained on the training visits, tested on 73,152 visits never seen.

a) LightGBM LambdaRank (gradient-boosted trees) - main feature-based ranker.
b) Logistic regression - fully explainable: score = sum of
   (weight x feature), so each pick splits exactly into its reasons.
c) Neural NRMS-style recommender, trained on the GPU (RTX 3050):
   - news encoder: MiniLM sentence embedding + category embedding
   - user encoder: self-attention over your last 50 reads
   - trained on 189,519 clicks, 4 epochs, ~8 minutes
d) Hybrid: LightGBM using the neural score as an extra feature.
e) Classic ML models on the same features: KNN classifier (k=50), linear SVM,
   kernel SVM (RBF via Nystroem approximation), random forest (200 trees).

Features the models use (plain-language labels for the UI):
  - Topic match with your history
  - Text similarity to your history
  - Share of your reads in this category / subcategory
  - Click-through rate on earlier days (popularity)
  - Times shown to anyone before now
  - Hours since the article first appeared (freshness)
  - How much history you have
  - Number of articles shown together

What the trees rely on most: earlier-day CTR (15%), article category (12%),
subcategory affinity (12%), freshness (10%), text similarity (10%).


8. RESULTS (measured on 73,152 unseen visits; 95% CI about +/-0.2)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Neural NRMS (GPU, MiniLM news encoder)   63.14  29.75  32.20   38.64
Linear SVM                               63.74  30.56  33.52   39.59
LightGBM LambdaRank                      63.83  30.58  33.26   39.46
Logistic regression                      63.84  30.56  33.54   39.61
Hybrid: LightGBM + neural score          66.60  32.05  35.47   41.64
Paper's model (BATM-NR, Table 5)         68.13  32.62  36.36   42.46   <- from the paper, not re-run

The app ranks with the logistic regression (every pick splits exactly into its reasons).
The hybrid is the most accurate model; it needs the neural encoder, so it is reported, not run in the browser.

Metric meanings (for tooltips):
  AUC      how often a clicked story ranks above an unclicked one (50 = coin flip)
  MRR      how high the first clicked story sits, on average
  nDCG@k   how much of the good stuff lands in the top k


9. PAGES TO DESIGN
------------------
A. Landing page (logged out)
   1. Nav: logo, How it works, Results, Data, Demo, [Sign in]
   2. Hero: headline + subtext + [Sign in] [Try a demo]; visual = tilted
      mosaic of real news "front page" cards (photo + category + headline)
   3. Who it's for (4 audiences)
   4. How it works (5 steps)
   5. What makes it different (bento: exact explanations / learned topics /
      you can push back / measured not claimed)
   6. Results (table + "what the model listens to" chart)
   7. The data (stats band + citation)
   8. Sign in + demo readers (Google / email / guest; 3 demo personas)
   9. FAQ
  10. Footer (paper + dataset credits, image credits)

B. Sign-in / onboarding
   - Sign in card: Continue with Google, Continue with email, Continue as guest
   - Interest picker: "What do you like to read?" pick 2-5 categories

C. Dashboard (logged in) - "For you"
   - Top bar: tabs (For you, Topics, Compare, Results, About), avatar, sign out
   - Greeting: "Good evening, <name>." + number of new picks + top topics
   - Feed cards: rank, match %, category, image, headline, summary, topic
     chips, one-line "Why", actions (More / Less / Not interested / Mark read /
     Why am I seeing this?)
   - Expanded Why panel: score split by signal (bars to 100%), past reads
     behind it, matched words highlighted, "what this doesn't know"
   - Sidebar: Your taste, Recently read, Hidden, What changed, How your feed
     is ranked (model card)

D. Topics (Topic Explorer): every learned topic with top words, your share,
   0x-2x slider, mute toggle, reset.
E. Compare: baseline vs TopicLens rankings side by side.
F. Results: full metrics table with confidence intervals, feature importance.
G. About: the paper, what's inherited vs new, limitations.


10. DESIGN DIRECTION (newsprint sketchbook)
--------------------------------------------
- Mood: hand-drawn notes on newsprint. Off-white newspaper-texture
  background (public/paper.png), black ink, one blue ink accent (#2449c4).
- Type: Caveat Brush for headings and the wordmark, Patrick Hand for
  handwritten body text, Domine (newspaper serif) for article headlines.
- Paper cards with torn edges and a soft drop shadow, slightly rotated.
- Black-and-white (grayscale) news photos inside "clipping" cards.
- Doodles: circled words ("shows its (working.)"), scribbled underlines,
  curved arrows, crown / star / spark marks, ink-blob step numbers,
  "= TopicLens =" wordmark with ink rays.
- Buttons: solid black brush button (primary), hand-drawn outline (secondary).
- Sections follow the three mockups: Landing hero, Who it's for (4 cards,
  blue ink), How it works (5-step zigzag with dotted path).


11. VOICE & COPY RULES
----------------------
- Plain, specific, honest. Numbers instead of adjectives.
- Never claim accuracy we haven't measured.
- No em dashes; short sentences.
